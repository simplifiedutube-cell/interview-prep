12.2Why hybrid retrieval for this corpus specifically?›
Strong answer contains
  • Legal text is exact-match-heavy: section numbers ("s 41"), defined terms, acronyms — BM25 territory. Users also ask in plain language — dense territory. Hybrid + RRF captures both; a purely dense system whiffs on "Rule 4.2.1."
12.3Explain reciprocal rank fusion. Why RRF over weighted score combination?›
Strong answer contains
  • RRF merges by rank position (score = Σ 1/(k + rank)), which sidesteps the problem that BM25 scores and cosine similarities live on incomparable scales. No score normalization, few knobs, robust default.
12.4SQLite for a multi-tenant system — really? Defend it, and tell me when you'd migrate off.TRAP›
Strong answer contains
  • Defense: single-node read-heavy workload, FTS5 built in, zero ops, perfect for a demo/small-firm scale; predicate isolation works identically.
  • Migration trigger: concurrent writers, horizontal scale, HA requirements → Postgres (+pgvector), same query shape. Knowing the exit proves the entry was a choice.
12.5The Act says one thing, the firm's internal procedure says another. What should the assistant answer?TRAP›
What they're testingDomain judgment. Compliance tools that silently pick a side are dangerous. Strong answer contains
  • Surface both, labeled by source, flag the conflict explicitly, and cite — never silently merge. Authority hierarchy (Act > Rules > guidance > firm procedure) shown to the user, with a recommendation to consult compliance.
12.6What are the risks of an LLM assistant in a regulated compliance domain, and what did you do about them?›
Strong answer contains

Cross-cutting traps & design prompts

Questions that span the whole resume — the ones a strong interviewer saves for the last twenty minutes.

17.1You've now built three retrieval systems (KNS RAG, AML assistant, tagging). Give me your personal RAG playbook: what do you always do, and what do you never do?DEEP-DIVE›

Always: eval set before tuning, hybrid retrieval by default for exact-term domains, citations, hard isolation below the model, regression gates. Never: trust one metric, ship without a refusal path, put security in the prompt. Having a codified playbook = senior.

17.2When would you NOT use RAG? Not use an agent? Not use an LLM at all?TRAP›

Small stable corpus that fits in context → no RAG. Single deterministic step → no agent. Rules/regex/classifier solves it → no LLM (your tagging and ML-platform work are proof you live this). The "when not" answer is worth more than any "how."

17.3Fine-tuning is absent from your resume. Why — and when would you reach for it?TRAP›

Own it: your problems were knowledge and orchestration problems (RAG/agents territory), not behavior-shaping at scale. Then show fluency: fine-tune (LoRA/QLoRA) for consistent format/tone, latency at scale, or distilling to a smaller model — never for injecting facts. Offer to whiteboard the LoRA setup to prove it's a choice, not a gap.

17.4Design [their product]'s AI feature on the whiteboard, using anything from your background.DEEP-DIVE›

Use one frame every time: Input → Retrieval → Generation → Verification → Feedback. Ask 2–3 clarifying questions first, lead with product metrics, name a failure mode per stage, end with the eval plan. Rehearse against the actual company's product before each loop.

17.5Estimate the monthly cost of your KNS assistant at 10× current usage — right now, out loud.METRIC›

The drill: queries/day × tokens/query (in+out) × price, ×30. Then the mitigation ladder in priority order: cache → route → trim → batch. Practice until the arithmetic is automatic under pressure.

17.6Your stack is Python + TypeScript. Which are you weaker in, and what would we see in your first code review?›

Honest, specific, small: name a real habit gap (e.g. advanced TS generics, or a Python pattern you over/under-use) plus how you catch it. Claiming equal mastery of both reads as unreflective.

17.7What's the most over-hyped thing in AI engineering right now, and the most under-rated?›

Have a genuine opinion with a reason from your own work — e.g. over-hyped: multi-agent everything; under-rated: deterministic verification and boring eval discipline (which happens to be your specialty — steer here).

17.8What questions do you have for us?›

Prepare five per company, at least two only answerable by someone who read their eng blog. Strong defaults: "How do you evaluate your AI features today — golden sets or vibes?" · "What's the worst production incident your AI system has had?" · "Where does this role sit between building and client-facing work?"
