Question Bank · 5 Aug 2026, 15:00–16:00 IST

Scope note. Your existing ai-engineer-interviews/real-questions.md is calibrated for frontier-lab and AI-native product loops (Anthropic, Sierra, Harvey, Glean). This round is different and generally easier: an India ATCI lateral “Skills Interview” for Custom Software Engineer, one hour, one or two interviewers, submitted via BSL Placement. Expect breadth over depth — Python and backend fundamentals first, GenAI second, one light design question, and resume probing throughout. Do not walk in optimised for a research-lab bar; walk in able to explain ordinary engineering cleanly.

The Accenture-attested verbatim questions below are marked [ACN-verbatim] and come from your own corpus (Accenture, Dec 2025 first-person accounts). Those are the highest-value items on this page.

Tier 1 — Prepare cold. Highest probability.

Python & backend

  1. Difference between a list and a tuple; when would you use each. (Near-universal opener in Indian service-company technical rounds.)
  2. Mutable vs immutable types — and the classic trap: what happens when you use a mutable default argument.
  3. Decorators — what they are, and one you’ve actually written.
  4. Generators vs lists; when memory matters. (Ties to your 1M-events/day pipeline.)
  5. *args / **kwargs.
  6. What is the GIL, and what does it mean for concurrency in Python.
  7. asyncio vs threading vs multiprocessing — and which you’d pick for an I/O-bound API. (FastAPI makes this near-certain.)
  8. Shallow vs deep copy.
  9. Exception handling: try/except/else/finally, and custom exceptions.
  10. List comprehension vs map/filter.

FastAPI / API design

  1. Why FastAPI over Flask or Django — you claim 4 years on it, so have a real answer, not “it’s fast.”
  2. How Pydantic validation works, and what you gain from it.
  3. Dependency injection in FastAPI — what you use it for.
  4. async def vs def endpoints — what actually happens differently.
  5. REST design: status codes, versioning, pagination, idempotency.
  6. Authentication — JWT/OAuth2 flow at a high level.
  7. How would you handle a long-running request (background tasks, queues).

SQL & data

  1. JOIN types; write a query with a GROUP BY and a HAVING.
  2. Indexes — what they cost, when they don’t get used.
  3. N+1 query problem and how you fix it in SQLAlchemy.
  4. Transactions and ACID; what isolation level problems look like in practice.

GenAI / RAG — your differentiator

  1. “Explain how RAG works, end to end.” [ACN-verbatim] — the single most-asked question in your entire corpus. Must be crisp.
  2. “What are you storing in the vector database?” [ACN-verbatim] — they want: embeddings + the chunk text + metadata for filtering. Say all three.
  3. “What are embeddings?” / “How does chunking happen?” [ACN-verbatim]
  4. “What is the key difference between a Generative AI system and an Agentic AI system?” [ACN-verbatim]
  5. “Have you heard about MCP?” [ACN-verbatim] — know the control model: tools are model-controlled, resources app-controlled, prompts user-controlled.
  6. “Which framework did you use — LangChain or CrewAI — and why?” [ACN-verbatim] — answer honestly from what you actually used; do not claim CrewAI depth you don’t have.
  7. When do you fine-tune vs RAG vs prompt engineering.
  8. Your RAG returns the right chunks but the answer still hallucinates — fix it.
  9. How do you evaluate a RAG pipeline. (Faithfulness, answer relevance, context precision/recall.)

Tier 2 — Likely. Have the shape ready.

  1. Temperature, top-k, top-p.
  2. What is a context window; what happens when you exceed it.
  3. Why do hallucinations happen.
  4. Hybrid search — BM25 + dense, and what RRF does. (Your AML/CTF project is the answer here.)
  5. Reranking — why, and what it costs you in latency.
  6. How do you refresh embeddings when source documents change.
  7. Which vector DB and why; what were the alternatives. (You have real Pinecone production experience — lead with that, not the hobby projects.)
  8. How does an agent decide which tool to call; what happens when a tool fails.
  9. How do you stop an agent looping forever.
  10. Prompt injection — through retrieved content or tool output.
  11. Docker: image vs container, what goes in a Dockerfile, why you’d use compose. (Your air-gapped build work is unusually strong here.)
  12. CI/CD — what your pipeline actually ran.
  13. Git: merge vs rebase, resolving a conflict, what you’d do about a bad commit on main.
  14. Unit vs integration tests; what you test in an LLM system specifically.

Tier 3 — Design & scenario. One of these is likely.

  1. Design a document Q&A system for an enterprise with 10,000+ documents and per-user permissions. (This is your day job plus your tenant-isolation work — the strongest possible draw.)
  2. Design a REST API for a booking/ticketing system.
  3. Scale a RAG system from 10k to 10M documents — what breaks first. (Use the 6-move template in story-bank.md. The answer is never “the LLM.”)
  4. How would you debug an API that got slow in production but is fine in staging.
  5. How would you add caching to reduce LLM cost. (You have the −30% cost claim; this is an invitation to use it.)

Tier 4 — Behavioural / HR. Short answers, then stop talking.

  1. Walk me through your resume. (90 seconds. Not five minutes.)
  2. Why are you looking to change.
  3. Why Accenture / why a services company after product work. (Have a real answer. This one catches people who obviously only want a raise.)
  4. Tell me about a difficult bug and how you found it.
  5. A time you disagreed with a technical decision.
  6. Biggest project you’ve owned end to end.
  7. Where do you see yourself in three years.
  8. Are you comfortable with client-facing work, shifts, relocation, return-to-office. (Accenture asks. Know your answer before it’s asked.)
  9. Notice period and expected CTC. (One number, not a range — see story-bank.md.)

The three questions most likely to hurt you

“You say 8+ years but your B.Tech is 2014.” One consistent number tied to 2016, one line, no defensiveness. Script it before you log in.

“You were at Turing and KNS at the same time.” 25 seconds, volunteered before they find it. Your answers/metric-defence.md has the script — read it once today.

“How exactly did you measure that 40% latency reduction?” Every number on your resume is a live grenade if you can’t state metric definition, baseline, n, and confounder in 30 seconds. See the claim-defence sheet.

Ask them at the end (have two ready)

  • What does the delivery team look like, and what would the first project be?
  • Is this role building GenAI systems for clients, or maintaining existing applications? (A real question, and it signals you’re evaluating them too.)
